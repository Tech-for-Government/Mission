# Project Cities: A Democratic Vision for New Urban Futures

## Executive Summary

This white paper introduces **Project Cities**—a radically democratic and decentralized framework for building new cities. In contrast to corporate or authoritarian mega-projects, Project Cities prioritize people, not profit; deliberation, not dictation. These are not utopias imposed from above, but collaborative efforts that grow from the ground up, driven by a shared vision of justice, equity, and sustainability.

The global crises we face—spiraling inequality, ecological collapse, housing unaffordability, civic fragmentation—require not only new policies but new places. Cities are more than infrastructure; they are systems of belonging, cooperation, and imagination. This document presents a compelling rationale for the creation of new cities, outlines systemic failures of the existing urban paradigm, and proposes a simple formula for democratic urban creation.

---

## I. The Case for New Cities

### A. Modern Challenges Require Structural Innovation

Cities across the world are failing to meet the basic needs of their people. Problems like housing unaffordability, urban congestion, climate vulnerability, and unequal service provision are becoming endemic—not just in the Global North, but everywhere:

- **Hong Kong:** Median home prices are 20 times the median income.
- **Lagos:** Nearly 70% of residents live in informal settlements without basic infrastructure.
- **San Francisco:** Over 8,000 people are homeless despite some of the world's highest housing costs.
- **Delhi:** Air quality regularly exceeds safe limits by 10x or more, with little room to escape in the dense sprawl.
- **London:** Rent consumes over 50% of income for many young professionals.
- **Jakarta:** Sinking under the weight of development and groundwater extraction, while millions lack sewage.
- **Paris:** Over-regulated development pushes working-class families to the periphery, weakening civic cohesion.
- **Cape Town:** Apartheid-era spatial inequality remains entrenched.
- **Toronto:** Home prices doubled in under a decade, despite aggressive supply-side policies.
- **São Paulo:** Transit deserts and zoning laws trap millions in low-opportunity environments.

Globally:
- **Over 1 billion people** live in slums or informal settlements.
- **70% of global carbon emissions** originate in cities.
- **Half the urban population** lacks safe access to public transport.

These failures are not marginal—they are systemic. They reflect an urban world captured by capital and calcified by bureaucracy.

### B. Cities as the Deep Architecture of Human Flourishing

Cities are humanity’s oldest social technology for cooperation and governance. But their modern instantiation is failing. Project Cities reimagine urban life not just as a product of policy but as a democratic act—an expression of shared values made physical.

---

## II. The Political Question: Project Cities vs. Authoritarian Urbanism

### A. The Rise of Closed Cities

The dominant trend in new city development today is authoritarian and corporate. Projects like Saudi Arabia’s NEOM, China’s Xiong’an, or the UAE’s Masdar City prioritize image, central control, and elite economic interests over civic life. They are:

- **Centrally planned** with little public input
- **Privatized** at nearly every level
- **Surveilled** and automated for control, not care

These cities reflect a belief that progress must be imposed.

### B. Project Cities as Democratic Urbanism

Project Cities offer an alternative: decentralized, pluralistic, and democratic. They are not franchises or brands, but frameworks. Local communities, professionals, and dreamers collaborate in shared governance.

They are pragmatic movements, not utopias. They recognize that democracy isn’t merely a system—it’s a practice, one that must be built into the bones of a place.

---

## III. The Project Cities Formula

To build a city, you need three things:

1. **Land** — with clear governance and public purpose
2. **Infrastructure** — combining capital, enterprise, and physical assets
3. **Workers** — encompassing both labor and the civic identity of its citizens

**The Project Cities Society** is a proposed global movement and institutional framework that seeks to facilitate the democratic provision of these three ingredients. It will:

- Acquire and steward land for public-oriented development
- Build civic infrastructure and open platforms (legal, digital, and physical)
- Organize people into engaged contributors, activists, and city-builders

Its core initiatives include:

- **Sample laws and governance templates** for self-determined communities
- **Collaborative tech tools** for participatory budgeting, voting, and deliberation
- **Regional and digital circles** for dialogue, skill exchange, and co-design
- **Committees of correspondence and action**, connecting local and global movements
- **A venture and grant hub**, supporting civic-aligned enterprises and cooperatives

Project Cities are not just built—they are lived into existence, through organizing, experimenting, and iterating.

---

## IV. Civic Foundations of Project Cities

Project Cities rest on these shared values:

1. **Democracy** — participatory, transparent, and polycentric governance
2. **Equity** — shared access to housing, work, education, and healthcare
3. **Pluralism** — welcoming diversity in all its forms
4. **Transparency** — data, algorithms, and finances open to scrutiny
5. **Sustainability** — both ecological and institutional resilience
6. **Community** — prioritizing care, safety, and human connection

These cities aren’t imposed—they’re composed.

---

## V. Who Project Cities Are For

This movement invites participation from across society:

- **Young people** priced out of cities but rich in ideas
- **Designers and builders** who want to create with conscience
- **Technologists and coders** seeking meaning in their work
- **Doctors and healthcare workers** dreaming of care-centered systems
- **Educators** disillusioned with bureaucratic, underfunded institutions
- **Manufacturers and farmers** ready to contribute to real economies
- **Organizers and local leaders** seeking autonomy and vision
- **Artists and cultural workers** who shape place through story
- **Retirees and caretakers** with deep time and social capital

Everyone has a role. Cities need more than capital—they need people.

---

## VI. Call to Action: Building the Movement

This white paper is not a blueprint for instant cities. Movements come from baby steps, and it should first and foremost be a **white paper about an ideological, political, and ultimately practical vision for a type of future**; it is not necessarily a call in and of itself for building new cities, but a charter of a movement.

To advance that movement, we propose:

### 1. **Committees of Correspondence and Action for Project Cities**
Decentralized, grassroots organizing groups embedded in local and virtual communities. These will:
- Host workshops and salons
- Develop pilot projects
- Engage in local political education

### 2. **The Project Cities Society**
A nonprofit and distributed platform to provide tools, land, funding, and community infrastructure for those building cities democratically.

### 3. **Global Communication and Cultural Strategy**
Stories, documentaries, mockups, and art that make the vision of Project Cities emotionally compelling and understandable—reaching across ideologies and professions.

Let it begin not in tech labs or state rooms, but in libraries, living rooms, community centers, and message boards. Movements don’t require permission. They require belief, effort, and each other.

---

## Conclusion

We live in a time of failing cities, fragmented economies, and exhausted institutions. But in every breakdown lies a seed of possibility.

Project Cities are that seed—growing from a simple formula: land, infrastructure, and people. By providing those democratically, we create not just places, but possibilities.

This is a call for architects of all kinds—whether you build with code, steel, words, or care—to join a project that is bigger than any one city.

Let us begin the civic century.
